# Lotus Garden — presentation build

```bash
npm install
npm run web
```

Open the forwarded port (usually 3000). This build intentionally has no service worker and no generated HTML source injection, so navigation, checkboxes, themes, music, quick add, and palettes remain responsive.

For years, I associated service with a designated time and place. It meant volunteering through National Charity League: arriving at a philanthropy, completing a shift, and recording the hours afterward. Yet some of my most meaningful contributions have happened without a scheduled shift at all: mentoring incoming state officers, creating technical communities where young women could develop a stronger sense of belonging, and using tutoring to help students regain confidence in their own abilities. Now, I consider anything that strengthens the people and communities around me to be for the common good, and I have learned to contribute by using what I already possess—my time, knowledge, relationships, and abilities—where they can be most useful.

My tutoring business has shown me how profoundly one person can affect another’s confidence in their own abilities. Across more than fifty students, I have learned that the problem in front of me is rarely just a missed equation. Sometimes, it is a student who has spent years believing they are simply “not a math person.” In those moments, explaining the material is only part of my responsibility. I have to identify where understanding broke down, set up more personalized approaches, and rebuild enough confidence for the student to attempt the next problem independently. One student who had struggled academically for years eventually earned an A on a test; another progressed from standard to honors mathematics. Their successes represent small improvements to my community: students approaching their education with greater confidence in what they can accomplish.

Beyond one-on-one impact, I have learned that my contributions can extend further when they create opportunities for others to grow, lead, and serve. Through Girls Who Code and Society of Women Engineers, I have created technical environments where young women can find an accessible entry point, develop their abilities, and form relationships with peers who encourage them to remain in competitive spaces. As a two-time Texas TSA State Officer, I have used the experience from my first term to mentor incoming officers through their own goals and responsibilities. Through National Charity League, I connected my chapter with a local philanthropy that ultimately generated more than 1,100 service hours, extending one connection into opportunities for an entire chapter to serve. Across each organization, what I could contribute became more valuable when it gave others greater opportunities to contribute themselves.

At Kentucky, I want to continue contributing in the ways I already know while discovering new ones. Years of tutoring could become peer tutoring through The Study Hub, while my experience building communities for women in technology could continue through the Association for Computing Machinery for Women. The Student Philanthropy Board particularly interests me as an opportunity to build on my experience connecting others with meaningful service, this time by helping more students discover where their own time and abilities could be useful. Beyond Kentucky, I want this same responsibility to grow alongside me. As my education and career give me greater knowledge, relationships, influence, and resources, I want to become increasingly deliberate about putting them to use for others. The common good is not one form of service I intend to continue; it is the standard I want to apply to whatever I have to offer.
